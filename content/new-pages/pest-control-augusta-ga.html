Augusta is the hub of the CSRA, and the pest pressure here follows the city’s shape. A consolidated city-county government since 1996, it stretches from the historic downtown on the Savannah River out to suburban neighborhoods and Fort Eisenhower’s edge. That means three very different jobs: older brick and frame buildings near the river and the Augusta Canal, a large hospital and university district, and post-war and newer subdivisions to the west and south.

What drives pest problems in Augusta?

The river and the canal. The Augusta Canal and the Savannah River corridor give Norway rats exactly what they need — soft banks, storm drains and food from the restaurants and dumpsters of downtown and Broad Street. Burrow systems under dumpster pads and along retaining walls are the source of most of the rats people see inside. Our detection dogs map which burrows are active before anything is treated.

Travel and turnover. Augusta fills up every April for the Masters, and the city sees steady traffic from the medical campuses, Fort Eisenhower moves and apartment turnover the rest of the year. All of that moves bed bugs. A certified canine screen tells a hotel manager, landlord or homeowner exactly which rooms have live activity, so treatment goes where it is needed.

Older housing stock. Neighborhoods such as Summerville, Harrisburg and the areas around downtown have crawlspaces, old foundations and mature trees — roof rats on the rooflines, squirrels in attics and American cockroaches coming up from crawlspaces and drains.

Who regulates pest control on the Georgia side?

Crossing the Savannah River changes the rulebook. In South Carolina, structural pest control is licensed through Clemson University's Department of Pesticide Regulation; in Georgia it runs through the Georgia Department of Agriculture's Structural Pest Control program and the Structural Pest Control Commission. Our Georgia work is done under Georgia credentials, and you can ask any company — us included — to show its Georgia company license before work starts.
