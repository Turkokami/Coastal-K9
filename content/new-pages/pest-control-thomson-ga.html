What drives pest problems in Thomson?

Commercial and industrial sites. Plants, warehouses and distribution buildings near I-20 deal with rodents, birds roosting in the steel and audit requirements. Our K9 rodent detection and bird exclusion programs are built for exactly that, with documentation for audits.

Historic homes. Older houses in and around downtown have crawlspaces and roofline gaps — rodents, squirrels and bats find them.

Rural land. Farms and wooded lots bring wildlife, ticks and fire ants to the edge of the yard.

Who regulates pest control on the Georgia side?

Crossing the Savannah River changes the rulebook. In South Carolina, structural pest control is licensed through Clemson University's Department of Pesticide Regulation; in Georgia it runs through the Georgia Department of Agriculture's Structural Pest Control program and the Structural Pest Control Commission. Our Georgia work is done under Georgia credentials, and you can ask any company — us included — to show its Georgia company license before work starts.
