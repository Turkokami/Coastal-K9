What drives pest problems in Martinez?

Mature canopy. Decades-old oaks and pines give roof rats and gray squirrels a highway onto rooflines. Attic noise is the classic sign — the fix is the roofline and the limbs, plus sealing every entry once the animals are out.

Water and lawns. Neighborhood ponds, golf courses and open lawns near the river draw resident Canada geese. Our dogs move geese off HOA ponds and common areas without harming them, timed around nesting season.

Aging construction. Homes from the 1970s and 1980s often have crawlspaces, settled slabs and worn door sweeps — easy entry for mice, American cockroaches and moisture pests like silverfish.

Who regulates pest control on the Georgia side?

Crossing the Savannah River changes the rulebook. In South Carolina, structural pest control is licensed through Clemson University's Department of Pesticide Regulation; in Georgia it runs through the Georgia Department of Agriculture's Structural Pest Control program and the Structural Pest Control Commission. Our Georgia work is done under Georgia credentials, and you can ask any company — us included — to show its Georgia company license before work starts.
