What drives pest problems in Evans?

Building into habitat. When woods and fields become subdivisions, the rodents, fire ants, armadillos and moles that lived there don’t leave — they move to the new lawns and foundations. Fire ant mounds in fresh sod and armadillo rooting in new beds are typical in new subdivisions; both are handled across the whole yard, not one hole at a time.

Ponds and golf communities. Stormwater ponds and courses throughout Evans attract resident Canada geese, and nesting pairs can make common areas unusable in spring. Dog hazing moves them on humanely.

Families and pets. Ticks and mosquitoes along wooded edges and stormwater ponds are a common summer problem in Evans. Seasonal yard treatments plus removing standing water make the yard usable again.

Who regulates pest control on the Georgia side?

Crossing the Savannah River changes the rulebook. In South Carolina, structural pest control is licensed through Clemson University's Department of Pesticide Regulation; in Georgia it runs through the Georgia Department of Agriculture's Structural Pest Control program and the Structural Pest Control Commission. Our Georgia work is done under Georgia credentials, and you can ask any company — us included — to show its Georgia company license before work starts.
