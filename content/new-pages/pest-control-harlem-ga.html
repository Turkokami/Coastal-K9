Harlem is a small city in western Columbia County, best known as the birthplace of Oliver Hardy of Laurel and Hardy — the town keeps a museum and holds a festival in his honor every fall. Around the historic downtown it’s a mix of older homes, newer subdivisions and open rural land.

What drives pest problems in Harlem?

Rural edges. Fields, woods and farm buildings mean mice and rats in sheds and garages in the fall, armadillos rooting lawns, and raccoons and opossums under porches.

Older downtown buildings. Historic storefronts and older homes have gaps at foundations, rooflines and utility entries — rodent, bat and squirrel entry points that need proper exclusion.

Ticks and fire ants. Wooded edges and pasture land bring lone star ticks into yards, and fire ants build fast in open lawns. Both are handled with seasonal, whole-property treatments.

Who regulates pest control on the Georgia side?

Crossing the Savannah River changes the rulebook. In South Carolina, structural pest control is licensed through Clemson University's Department of Pesticide Regulation; in Georgia it runs through the Georgia Department of Agriculture's Structural Pest Control program and the Structural Pest Control Commission. Our Georgia work is done under Georgia credentials, and you can ask any company — us included — to show its Georgia company license before work starts.
