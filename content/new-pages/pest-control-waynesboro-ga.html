What drives pest problems in Waynesboro?

Agriculture. Grain, feed, equipment sheds and barns are where rats and mice multiply. Norway rat burrow systems around grain and feed storage are a specialty of ours — the dogs find the active burrows and harborage before anything is treated.

Kennels and working dogs. Kennel buildings and feed rooms attract rodents and flies; we work around animals with targeted, documented treatments.

Older downtown and homes. Historic buildings have the roofline and foundation gaps that let in bats, squirrels and rodents.

How a Coastal K9 visit works

  1. Inspect

    A certified detection dog and handler find where the problem actually is — inside walls, under slabs, in specific rooms.

  2. Pinpoint and document

    The handler confirms what the dog found and writes it up, so you know exactly what is being treated and why.

  3. Treat, seal and verify

    Targeted treatment only where activity is confirmed, exclusion so it doesn’t come back, and follow-up to prove it.

Who regulates pest control on the Georgia side?

Crossing the Savannah River changes the rulebook. In South Carolina, structural pest control is licensed through Clemson University's Department of Pesticide Regulation; in Georgia it runs through the Georgia Department of Agriculture's Structural Pest Control program and the Structural Pest Control Commission. Our Georgia work is done under Georgia credentials, and you can ask any company — us included — to show its Georgia company license before work starts.
