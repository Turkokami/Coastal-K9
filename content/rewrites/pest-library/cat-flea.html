Cat Flea — Identification & Control

Despite the name, the cat flea is the flea found on most dogs and cats in South Carolina — and the one that bites people around the ankles after a pet comes home with them. Most of a flea problem isn't on the pet at all; it's eggs, larvae and pupae in carpets, bedding and shady spots in the yard.

Quick answer: flea control works when the pet, the home and the yard are treated together. Treat only one and the others reinfest it.

How to identify cat fleas

FeatureWhat to look for
AdultsTiny (about 1/8 inch), dark reddish-brown, flattened side to side, jump readily
Flea dirtBlack specks in pet fur or bedding that turn reddish on a damp paper towel
Bites on peopleSmall itchy red bumps, usually around ankles and lower legs

Life cycle

Adult fleas live on the animal and lay eggs that fall off into carpet, furniture, pet bedding and shaded outdoor areas. Larvae hide deep in fibers and feed on debris and flea dirt, then spin cocoons. Pupae can wait inside the cocoon for weeks until vibration, warmth or carbon dioxide signals a host — which is why people walk into a "sudden" flea problem after returning from vacation or moving into a home where pets lived.

Prevention you can do

  • Keep every pet in the home on a veterinarian-recommended flea product.
  • Vacuum often, especially pet resting areas, and empty the vacuum outside.
  • Wash pet bedding in hot water weekly.
  • Limit stray cats and wildlife (raccoons, opossums) under decks and crawlspaces.

Frequently asked questions

Why am I still seeing fleas after treatment?

Pupae in their cocoons aren't affected by most treatments and keep emerging for a couple of weeks. Keep vacuuming — it helps trigger them to hatch into treated areas.
