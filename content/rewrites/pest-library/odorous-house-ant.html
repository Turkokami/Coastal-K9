Odorous House Ant — Identification & Control

The odorous house ant is the small dark ant that marches in long trails across kitchen counters, bathrooms and windowsills — especially after rain. Crush one and you'll notice why it has its name: a smell often compared to rotten coconut.

Quick answer: spraying the trail kills the workers you see and splits the colony into more nests. Slow-acting bait the workers carry home is what clears them.

How to identify the odorous house ant

FeatureWhat to look for
Size & colorAbout 1/16–1/8 inch, brown to black, all workers the same size
SmellA sour, rotten-coconut odor when crushed
TrailsLong, busy trails along edges, baseboards and pipes
Look-alikesArgentine ant (light brown, no odor), pavement ant, pharaoh ant (tiny, yellowish)

Behavior and biology

Colonies have many queens and spread across many nest sites — under mulch, stones and siding, in wall voids, under insulation and behind outlets. They feed heavily on sugary foods and on honeydew from aphids in landscaping, and they move indoors when rain floods outdoor nests or when weather turns hot and dry. When a colony is disturbed by repellent sprays it simply buds into new satellite nests.

Why they're a problem

They don't sting or damage structures, but large indoor trails contaminate food and counters and are one of the most common reasons people call about ants in the CSRA.

Prevention you can do

  • Wipe up sugary spills and keep sweet foods sealed.
  • Pull mulch back from the foundation and trim shrubs touching the house.
  • Seal cracks where trails enter — around windows, pipes and siding.
  • Don't spray trails; it scatters the colony.

Frequently asked questions

Why do ants show up after it rains?

Heavy rain floods shallow outdoor nests, and the colony moves to drier ground — often inside your walls.

How long does baiting take?

You'll usually see more ants on the bait for a few days, then a steady drop over one to two weeks as the bait reaches the queens.
