Black Widow Spider — Identification & Control

Black widows are the shiny black spiders with a red hourglass under the abdomen. Around Aiken they live in woodpiles, sheds, barns and tack rooms, crawlspaces, meter boxes, under outdoor furniture and in stacked pots — quiet, cluttered places people reach into with bare hands.

Quick answer: widow bites are uncommon and usually happen when a spider is pressed against skin — reaching into a glove, a box or a woodpile. Wear gloves in storage areas, and seek medical care for spreading pain, cramping or trouble breathing after a bite.

How to identify black widows

FeatureWhat to look for
Adult femaleGlossy black, round abdomen, about 1/2 inch body, red hourglass on the underside
WebMessy, irregular, very strong silk, usually low to the ground in sheltered spots
Egg sacsRound, tan to gray, papery, hung in the web
Look-alikesBrown widow (tan-brown, spiky egg sacs, increasingly common in SC), false widows

Behavior and biology

Widows are shy and stay in their webs. Females make several egg sacs over a season, each holding hundreds of eggs. South Carolina has the southern and northern black widow plus the introduced brown widow, which often nests on outdoor furniture and railings.

Prevention you can do

  • Wear gloves when moving firewood, pots, boxes and stored equipment.
  • Keep woodpiles off the ground and away from the house.
  • Reduce clutter in garages, sheds and barns; store items in sealed bins.
  • Shake out boots, gloves and gear stored outdoors.

Frequently asked questions

Are black widows aggressive?

No. They bite defensively, usually when trapped against skin.

What's the spider with spiky egg sacs?

That's likely the brown widow, a related species now common in South Carolina. Its egg sacs look like small spiky balls.
