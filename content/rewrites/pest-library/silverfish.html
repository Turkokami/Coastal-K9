Silverfish — Identification & Control

Silverfish are the shiny, carrot-shaped insects that dart out of bathtubs, sinks, basements, bookshelves and boxes of stored papers. They're harmless to people but feed on starches — paper, book bindings, wallpaper paste, cardboard, cereal and some fabrics — and they are a sign of moisture.

Quick answer: silverfish mean humidity. Fix the moisture and most of the problem goes with it.

How to identify silverfish

FeatureWhat to look for
BodyAbout 1/2 inch, wingless, flattened, tapered like a teardrop or carrot
ColorSilvery-gray, metallic scales
TailThree long bristles at the rear, two long antennae
Look-alikeFirebrat (mottled gray-brown, prefers hot spots like water heaters and boiler rooms)

Behavior and biology

Silverfish are nocturnal and need high humidity. They live for several years and can survive long periods without food, hiding in wall voids, under baseboards, in attics and in stored boxes, and coming out at night to feed.

Signs of silverfish

  • Irregular holes or surface grazing on paper, book covers, wallpaper and boxes.
  • Yellowish stains and tiny pepper-like droppings.
  • Insects trapped in sinks and tubs.

Prevention you can do

  • Run a dehumidifier in basements and fix leaks; vent bathrooms and dryers.
  • Store papers, books and clothing in sealed plastic bins.
  • Reduce cardboard storage and clutter.
  • Seal cracks along baseboards and around pipes.
