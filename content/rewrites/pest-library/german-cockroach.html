German Cockroach — Identification & Control

The German cockroach is the small, fast indoor roach found in kitchens, bathrooms and food-service areas. It is the cockroach that breeds inside buildings, multiplies faster than any other common species, and is the one most likely to show up in an apartment, restaurant or school.

Quick answer: small tan roaches with two dark stripes behind the head, seen in the kitchen or bathroom — especially in daylight — means an established German cockroach infestation. Sprays usually make it worse; baits, sanitation and crack-and-crevice work fix it.

Behavior and biology

German cockroaches live almost entirely indoors near warmth, moisture and food. Each egg case holds roughly 30–40 eggs, and because the female carries it until hatching, the eggs are protected from many treatments. Under good conditions the life cycle from egg to adult takes about two months, so populations grow very quickly.

They hide in tight cracks — hinges, the backs of cabinets, under sinks, inside appliance motors and electronics — and come out at night. Seeing them during the day usually means hiding places are already full.

Why German cockroaches are a problem

They contaminate food and surfaces, and their droppings, shed skins and saliva are well-documented allergens and asthma triggers, especially for children in multifamily housing. In restaurants and food handling they are an immediate health-inspection problem. Many populations have also developed resistance to common insecticides, and store-bought sprays and foggers tend to scatter them into neighboring units.

Signs of an infestation

  • Pepper-like droppings and dark smear marks in cabinet corners, hinges and behind appliances.
  • Shed skins and empty egg cases in cracks and under sinks.
  • Live roaches in daytime, or scattering when the light comes on.
  • An oily, musty smell in heavy infestations.

Prevention you can do

  • Don't leave dishes, food or pet bowls out overnight; wipe crumbs and grease.
  • Fix drips and leaks under sinks and around appliances.
  • Inspect grocery boxes, used appliances and furniture before bringing them in.
  • Seal gaps around plumbing and in cabinets; reduce cardboard and clutter.

Frequently asked questions

Why do roaches keep coming back after I spray?

Most sprays don't reach the egg cases or the cracks where most roaches hide, and repellent sprays push them into walls and next-door units. Baits that roaches carry back to the harborage work far better.

Do German cockroaches live outside?

Rarely. They depend on indoor warmth and food. If you're seeing large roaches outdoors, it's more likely an American or smoky-brown cockroach.
