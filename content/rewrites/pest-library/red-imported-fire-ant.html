Red Imported Fire Ant — Identification & Control

The red imported fire ant builds the domed dirt mounds that pop up across CSRA lawns, pastures, school fields and road shoulders after rain. Disturb one and hundreds of workers boil out and sting. It arrived in the U.S. through the port of Mobile, Alabama, and now covers all of South Carolina.

Quick answer: pouring something on the mounds you can see moves the colony a few feet away. Lasting control treats the whole yard with bait first, then spot-treats the mounds that remain.

How to identify fire ants

FeatureWhat to look for
WorkersReddish-brown with a darker abdomen, mixed sizes in one colony, about 1/8–1/4 inch
MoundsLoose, fluffy soil domes with no central opening on top; workers enter through underground tunnels
BehaviorSwarm aggressively and sting repeatedly when the mound is disturbed
StingsBurning sting followed by a white pustule a day later
Look-alikesNative fire ants and other mound builders; harmless ants usually leave a crater with a center hole

Behavior and biology

A fire ant colony can hold tens of thousands to a couple hundred thousand workers, plus one or more queens deep below the mound. Mounds rise after rain and in cool weather; in hot, dry spells colonies stay underground and forage through tunnels. Winged males and queens fly out in mating flights, mostly in spring and early summer, which is how new mounds appear across a yard.

Why fire ants are a problem

Stings are painful for everyone and dangerous for people who are allergic, and for small children, the elderly, pets and livestock. Mounds damage mowers, smother turf, and colonies nest in electrical boxes, AC units and pump housings, causing shorts.

The two-step method

  1. Broadcast bait across the whole property

    Foraging workers carry bait back to the queens, reaching colonies you can't see. Bait works best on dry ground when ants are actively foraging.

  2. Treat problem mounds directly

    Mounds near play areas, doorways and equipment are treated individually for faster knockdown.

Frequently asked questions

Does pouring boiling water on a mound work?

It kills some ants but rarely the queen, and the colony usually relocates nearby. It also kills the grass. Bait reaches the queen.

When is the best time to treat?

Spring and fall, when ants are foraging and soil temperatures are moderate, on a dry day with no rain expected.
