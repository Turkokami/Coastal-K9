Eastern Subterranean Termite — Identification & What To Do

The eastern subterranean termite is the most widespread termite in South Carolina. Colonies live in the soil and build mud tubes up foundations to reach the wood in a house, where they feed out of sight. Most homeowners first notice them when a swarm of winged termites appears indoors in spring.

Quick answer: winged insects swarming from a wall or window in spring, or pencil-width mud tubes on a foundation, mean you need a termite inspection soon. Coastal K9 doesn't treat termites in-house; we coordinate inspection and treatment through a licensed termite partner.

Signs of termites

  • Swarmers or piles of shed wings near windows and doors in spring.
  • Mud tubes on foundation walls, piers, crawlspace walls or inside wall cavities.
  • Damaged wood that sounds hollow or has galleries packed with soil.
  • Bubbling paint or soft spots in baseboards and trim.

Behavior and biology

Colonies can contain many thousands of termites and forage through the soil over a wide area. They need moisture, so leaks, poor drainage, wood touching soil and damp crawlspaces all raise the risk. South Carolina also has the invasive Formosan subterranean termite in parts of the state, which forms much larger colonies.

What to do

Termites are slow compared with fire or water damage, so you have time to get a proper inspection and compare options — but don't ignore them. Avoid spraying swarmers with store products; it does nothing to the colony.

Frequently asked questions

When do termites swarm in South Carolina?

Eastern subterranean termites usually swarm in late winter and spring, often on warm days after rain. Formosan termites swarm later, typically at dusk in late spring and early summer.
