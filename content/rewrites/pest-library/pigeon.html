Pigeon — Identification & Control

The rock pigeon (feral pigeon) is the ledge, beam and parapet bird of every CSRA downtown, shopping center, warehouse and parking deck. Pigeons nest on buildings rather than in trees, breed almost year-round, and stay loyal to a roost — which is why they come back no matter how many times they're scared off.

Quick answer: pigeons are solved by taking away the places they roost and nest — netting, spikes and wire on the exact ledges they use — not by noise or plastic owls.

How to identify the pigeon

FeatureWhat to look for
SizeAbout 12–13 inches long, plump
ColorTypically blue-gray with two dark wing bars and an iridescent green-purple neck; many color variations
WhereLedges, beams, signs, rooftop equipment, bridges, parking structures, loading docks
Look-alikesMourning doves (slimmer, tan, long pointed tail — a protected native species) and Eurasian collared-doves

Behavior and biology

Pigeons descend from cliff-nesting birds, so buildings suit them perfectly. A pair raises two chicks at a time and can nest several times a year. They gather in flocks around reliable food — spilled grain, dumpsters, people feeding them — and roost and nest in the same sheltered spots day after day, building up droppings and nesting debris.

Why pigeons are a problem

  • Droppings stain and corrode masonry, metal and paint, and make walkways and loading docks slippery.
  • Health. Accumulated droppings can harbor fungi associated with histoplasmosis and cryptococcosis; clean-up of heavy deposits needs proper protection.
  • Nests and debris clog gutters, drains and vents, and attract mites and other insects.
  • Food safety. Birds inside warehouses and food facilities are an audit and contamination problem.

Signs of a pigeon problem

  • Droppings building up below ledges, beams, signs and rooftop units.
  • Feathers and nesting material on sills and in rafters.
  • Birds flushing from the same spot every time a door opens.

Prevention you can do

  • Stop feeding, and keep dumpsters closed and loading areas swept.
  • Close openings into warehouses, attics and parking-structure voids.
  • Clean droppings with water and protective gear rather than dry sweeping.

How we handle pigeons

We match the deterrent to the surface and the pressure: bird netting to close off beams, canopies and recesses; spikes and wire systems on ledges, signs and parapets; and exclusion of building openings. Frequently asked questions

Do plastic owls keep pigeons away?

Only briefly. Pigeons quickly learn that a decoy never moves. Physical exclusion — netting, spikes, wire — is what keeps them off a ledge long-term.

Are pigeons protected?

Feral rock pigeons are not protected under the federal Migratory Bird Treaty Act. Native doves and most other birds are, so it matters which bird you're dealing with.
