Eastern Gray Squirrel — Identification & Removal

Gray squirrels are everywhere in the CSRA's tree canopy — and when they find a gap at a roof return, gable vent or soffit, they move into attics to nest, usually in late winter and again in late summer.

Quick answer: running and scratching in the attic in the early morning and late afternoon is usually squirrels (rats are active at night). Get them out, then close the entry with metal they can't chew.

How to identify squirrel activity

FeatureWhat to look for
AnimalGray with a white belly and bushy tail, about 16–20 inches including tail
TimingActive during daylight, especially early morning
DamageChewed holes at roof edges, vents and soffits; gnawed wiring; shredded insulation nests
Look-alikesRoof rats (night, smaller pointed droppings), flying squirrels (night, very small)

Why squirrels in the attic are a problem

Squirrels chew constantly, including on electrical wiring, and their nests and droppings contaminate insulation. Once inside, they enlarge openings that let in water and other animals.

Prevention you can do

  • Trim limbs back from the roof.
  • Repair rotted fascia, soffits and roof returns.
  • Screen vents and chimneys with heavy metal mesh.

Frequently asked questions

Can I just close the hole?

Not until the squirrels are out. A trapped squirrel will chew a new hole, often into the living space, and young left inside will die in the attic.
