Carpenter Ant — Identification & Control

Carpenter ants are the large black (or red-and-black) ants that nest in wood. They don't eat it the way termites do — they carve smooth galleries into wood that is already damp or softened, which makes them a signal of a moisture problem as much as a pest problem.

Quick answer: large ants indoors at night and piles of fine sawdust-like "frass" mean a carpenter ant nest nearby. Find the nest and the moisture behind it.

How to identify carpenter ants

FeatureCarpenter antTermite swarmer (look-alike)
WaistNarrow, pinched waistThick, straight waist
AntennaeElbowedStraight, bead-like
Wings (swarmers)Front wings longer than hind wingsAll four wings equal length
SizeWorkers about 1/4–1/2 inch, varied sizesAbout 3/8 inch including wings
DamageSmooth, clean galleries; pushes out frassRough galleries packed with mud

Behavior and biology

A mature colony often has a parent nest outdoors — in a stump, dead limb or landscape timber — and satellite nests inside walls, window and door frames, porch columns and areas around leaks. Workers forage mostly at night along fences, limbs and utility lines, eating insects and honeydew. Winged swarmers emerge in spring.

Signs of carpenter ants

  • Large ants indoors, especially at night or in spring.
  • Small piles of frass — wood shavings mixed with insect parts — below a crack or hole.
  • Faint rustling inside a wall or door frame.
  • Winged ants inside in spring.

Frequently asked questions

Do carpenter ants eat wood?

No. They excavate it to nest and push the shavings out. Termites eat wood.

Are carpenter ants as damaging as termites?

Usually not, but long-term nests in wet wood can weaken framing — and the moisture that attracted them causes damage of its own.
