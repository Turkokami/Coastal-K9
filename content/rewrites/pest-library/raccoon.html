Raccoon — Identification & Removal

Raccoons are smart, strong and very good at getting into attics, chimneys, crawlspaces and under decks — especially females looking for a safe place to raise young in spring. In the CSRA they also raid trash, pet food and garden produce.

Quick answer: heavy thumping in the attic at night, torn soffits or roof vents, and scattered trash usually mean a raccoon. Don't corner or handle one — raccoons are a primary rabies carrier in South Carolina.

How to identify raccoon activity

FeatureWhat to look for
AnimalGray-brown, black "mask," ringed tail, 2–3 feet long including tail
TracksHand-like prints with five long toes
DamageTorn shingles, soffits, vent covers and screens; flattened insulation; dug-up lawns (grubbing)
DroppingsLarge, dark, often in one "latrine" spot on roofs, decks or attics

Why raccoons are a problem

Besides structural damage, raccoons can carry rabies and leave latrines that may contain raccoon roundworm eggs, which are hazardous to people. Trash raids and pet-food theft draw them back night after night.

Prevention you can do

  • Use locking or strapped trash lids and store cans in a garage or shed.
  • Feed pets indoors and remove fallen fruit.
  • Cap chimneys and screen vents with heavy-gauge metal.
  • Trim limbs that give access to the roof.

Frequently asked questions

Is a raccoon out in daytime rabid?

Not necessarily — mothers often forage in daylight in spring. But a raccoon that is staggering, unusually aggressive or unafraid should be avoided and reported to animal control.
