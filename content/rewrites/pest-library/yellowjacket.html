Yellowjacket — Identification & Control

Yellowjackets are the black-and-yellow wasps that guard ground nests in lawns and banks, nest inside wall voids and attics, and crowd around trash cans and picnic tables in late summer. In the CSRA their colonies peak in late summer and fall — exactly when people are mowing, raking and spending time outside.

Quick answer: don't pour gasoline in a ground nest or seal a wall nest from outside. A blocked colony can chew its way into the house. Have the nest treated at the entrance, usually after dark.

How to identify yellowjackets

NestHidden: in the ground, in wall voids, attics, shrubs; paper comb insidePaper wasp: open umbrella-shaped comb under eaves
TemperamentDefends the nest aggressively; can sting repeatedlyPaper wasps are less aggressive; honey bees sting once

Behavior and biology

Each colony starts in spring from a single queen and grows all summer, often to thousands of workers by late summer. Workers feed the young on insects, then switch to sweets and proteins in late season, which is when they show up at drinks and garbage. In most of South Carolina colonies die out in winter, though some southern colonies can persist and grow very large.

Why yellowjackets are a problem

Disturbing a ground nest with a mower or string trimmer can trigger a mass attack. Multiple stings are dangerous, and for people with venom allergies a single sting can be an emergency. Wall-void nests sometimes break through drywall into living space.

Frequently asked questions

Should I plug the hole where they're going in?

No. Blocking a wall or attic nest from the outside can force the colony to find a new way out — sometimes into the house.

Why are yellowjackets so aggressive in the fall?

Colonies are at peak size and workers are hunting for sugar, so there are simply more of them around food and people.
