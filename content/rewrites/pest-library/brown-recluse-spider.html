Brown Recluse Spider — Identification & Control

The brown recluse is the spider South Carolinians worry about most and actually meet least. Verified specimens in the state are rare and scattered, and most "brown recluse" sightings turn out to be harmless look-alikes — usually the southern house spider. Correct identification comes before anything else.

Quick answer: count the eyes. A brown recluse has six eyes in three pairs; most spiders have eight. If you can, keep the spider (in a jar or sealed bag) so it can be identified.

How to identify a brown recluse

FeatureBrown recluseSouthern house spider (common look-alike)
EyesSix eyes in three pairsEight eyes clustered together
MarkingDark violin shape on the back, neck pointing toward the abdomenNo violin; males are tan and long-legged
Body & legsUniform tan to brown, no bands or spines on legs, fine hairs onlyUniform brown, long legs
WebSmall irregular retreat web, not used to catch preyFlat, tangled web radiating from a crack

Why identification matters

Research on South Carolina found very few confirmed brown recluse specimens statewide while physicians reported far more suspected bites — and many skin wounds blamed on spiders are actually bacterial infections such as MRSA. That's why we identify the spider first and never treat for a recluse that isn't there.

Behavior and biology

Where established, recluses hide in undisturbed, dry places — boxes, closets, behind furniture, in attics and garages — and hunt at night. They bite only when pressed against skin, typically in clothing, shoes or bedding.

Prevention you can do

  • Store items in sealed plastic bins rather than cardboard.
  • Move beds away from walls and keep bedding off the floor.
  • Shake out clothing and shoes stored in garages or closets.
  • Use sticky monitors along walls in storage areas to see what's present.

Frequently asked questions

Are brown recluses common in Aiken County?

Confirmed brown recluse records in South Carolina are uncommon. Most suspected recluses we see are other brown spiders.

What should I do if I think I was bitten?

See a doctor, and bring the spider if you can. Many wounds blamed on spiders are actually infections that need different treatment.
