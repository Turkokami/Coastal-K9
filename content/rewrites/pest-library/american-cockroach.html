American Cockroach — Identification & Control

The American cockroach is the big reddish-brown roach many South Carolinians call a "palmetto bug." It lives mostly outside the living space — in crawlspaces, sewers, drains, mulch beds and tree holes — and wanders indoors, especially after heavy rain or in the heat of summer.

Quick answer: one large roach in the house now and then is usually an outdoor American cockroach that got in. The fix is the crawlspace, drains and perimeter — not spraying the kitchen.

How to identify the American cockroach

FeatureWhat to look for
SizeOne of the largest house-invading roaches, about 1.5–2 inches
ColorShiny reddish-brown with a pale yellowish band around the shield behind the head
WingsFully winged as adults; can glide or fly short distances in warm weather
Look-alikesSmoky-brown cockroach (uniformly dark mahogany), Florida woods roach (very dark, short wings, strong odor), German cockroach (much smaller, two stripes)

Behavior and biology

American cockroaches favor warm, damp places: sewer systems, storm drains, crawlspaces, boiler rooms, mulch and leaf litter. The female drops or glues each egg case — about 16 eggs — in a sheltered spot. Development is slow compared with the German cockroach, and adults can live a year or more.

They come indoors through gaps under doors, around pipes, through floor drains and weep holes, and up from crawlspaces. Heavy rain and drought both push them toward buildings.

Why American cockroaches are a problem

Because they move between sewers, garbage and kitchens, they can carry bacteria onto food surfaces, and their droppings and body parts contribute to indoor allergens. In commercial kitchens and food plants they are an inspection issue on their own.

Signs of activity

  • Large roaches near drains, in bathrooms, garages and basements, often at night.
  • Droppings — larger, blunt-ended pellets — in crawlspaces and utility areas.
  • Egg cases about 3/8 inch long, dark brown, tucked into cracks.

Prevention you can do

  • Keep crawlspaces ventilated and dry; fix leaking pipes and AC condensate lines.
  • Pull mulch and leaf litter back from the foundation and keep gutters flowing.
  • Fit door sweeps, seal gaps around pipes and screen weep holes.
  • Keep drain covers in place and run water in seldom-used drains.

Frequently asked questions

Is a palmetto bug the same as a cockroach?

Yes. "Palmetto bug" is a regional name used for large outdoor cockroaches — most often the American cockroach, sometimes the smoky-brown or Florida woods roach.

Does one big roach mean my house is infested?

Not necessarily. An occasional American cockroach usually wandered in from outside. Several a week, or small roaches in the kitchen, point to a different problem.
