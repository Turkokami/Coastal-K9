House Sparrow — Identification & Control

The house sparrow is the small brown bird that nests in store signs, light fixtures, gutters, dock-door housings and warehouse rafters. It was introduced to North America in the 1850s, lives almost exclusively around people and buildings, and often gets inside retail stores, garden centers and food warehouses.

Quick answer: chirping birds nesting inside a sign letter, a light fixture or the steel above a loading door are usually house sparrows. Exclusion of those specific nesting gaps is what stops them.

How to identify the house sparrow

FeatureWhat to look for
SizeAbout 6 inches, chunky with a thick seed-eating bill
MaleGray crown, chestnut nape, black bib, white cheeks
FemalePlain buff-brown with a pale stripe behind the eye
Look-alikesNative sparrows and finches (protected) — check the bib and gray crown on males

Behavior and biology

House sparrows nest in almost any enclosed space on a building and can raise several broods a season. They are bold, social birds that follow food: spilled grain and seed, outdoor dining, garden centers and open warehouse doors. Once inside a large building they can live there year-round.

Why house sparrows are a problem

  • Inside buildings they contaminate stock and food areas — a serious issue for food warehouses, retail and food processing.
  • Nests in signs and light fixtures create fire risk and damage fixtures.
  • Droppings under nest sites and on merchandise, equipment and walkways.
  • Native birds. They compete aggressively with bluebirds and martins for nest boxes.

Frequently asked questions

Are house sparrows protected?

House sparrows are non-native and are not protected by the federal Migratory Bird Treaty Act. Native sparrows are, so correct identification comes first.

How do sparrows get inside a warehouse?

Mostly through dock and overhead doors left open, and through gaps at roof lines and wall penetrations. They nest in the steel and rafters once inside.
