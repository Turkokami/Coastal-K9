Paper Wasp — Identification & Control

Paper wasps build the open, umbrella-shaped paper combs that hang from eaves, porch ceilings, door frames, playground equipment and grill lids. They're beneficial predators of caterpillars, but nests over doorways and walkways lead to stings.

Quick answer: a small, open comb under the eaves with slender wasps on it is a paper wasp nest. Nests in high-traffic spots should be removed; nests out of the way can often be left alone.

How to identify paper wasps

FeatureWhat to look for
BodySlender, about 3/4–1 inch, long legs that dangle in flight
ColorBrown or reddish with yellow markings, or yellow-and-black in some species
NestSingle open comb of gray paper cells, hanging from a short stalk
Look-alikesYellowjackets (compact, hidden nests), mud daubers (thin-waisted, mud nests)

Behavior and biology

A fertilized queen starts each nest in spring; colonies stay fairly small, usually a few dozen wasps. Workers hunt caterpillars and other insects. In fall, new queens leave to overwinter in sheltered places — attics, wall voids, sheds — which is why paper wasps sometimes turn up indoors on warm winter days.

Frequently asked questions

Are paper wasps aggressive?

Less than yellowjackets. They usually sting only when the nest is bumped or threatened — which is why nests on doors and railings are the ones to remove.
