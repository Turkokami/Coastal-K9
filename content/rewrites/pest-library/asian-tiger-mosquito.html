Asian Tiger Mosquito — Identification & Control

The Asian tiger mosquito is the small black-and-white striped mosquito that bites during the day in shady CSRA yards. It breeds in tiny amounts of standing water — bottle caps, plant saucers, clogged gutters, tires, toys — so most of the mosquitoes biting you were born within a short distance of your house.

Quick answer: if you're getting bitten in the daytime around your own yard, the breeding source is probably on your property or your neighbor's. Dump standing water every week and treat the shady areas where adults rest.

How to identify the Asian tiger mosquito

FeatureWhat to look for
MarkingsBlack with bright white bands on the legs and a single white stripe down the head and back
SizeSmall, about 1/4 inch or less
When it bitesDaytime, especially morning and late afternoon, in shade
Look-alikesYellow fever mosquito (lyre-shaped markings on the back)

Behavior and biology

Females lay eggs just above the waterline in containers. The eggs can survive dry periods and hatch when the container refills, and in warm weather the trip from egg to biting adult can take about a week. Adults rest in shaded vegetation and usually don't fly far from where they emerged. The species was introduced into the U.S. in the 1980s and has spread across the Southeast.

Why it matters

Asian tiger mosquitoes are aggressive daytime biters that make yards unusable, and they are capable of transmitting several viruses. Your health department tracks mosquito-borne disease; reducing populations around the home cuts exposure.

Prevention you can do

  • Tip and drain every container weekly — saucers, buckets, toys, tarps, boat covers.
  • Clean gutters and fix low spots that hold water.
  • Change birdbath and pet water at least weekly.
  • Drill drain holes in tire swings and recycling bins.

Frequently asked questions

Do mosquito treatments last all season?

No single treatment does. Repeat treatments through the warm months plus removing standing water give the best results.
