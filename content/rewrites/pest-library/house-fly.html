House Fly — Identification & Control

House flies are the gray flies that buzz around kitchens, restaurants, dumpsters, barns and loading docks. They breed in decaying organic matter — garbage, manure, spilled feed, grease and food waste — and carry what they land on onto food and surfaces.

Quick answer: lots of flies means a breeding source nearby, usually within a short distance. Find and remove it, and keep flies out; traps and sprays alone won't keep up.

How to identify the house fly

FeatureWhat to look for
Size & colorAbout 1/4 inch, dull gray, four dark stripes on the thorax
Look-alikesBlow flies (metallic green or blue — dead animals), flesh flies (checkered abdomen), fruit and phorid flies (tiny, drains and fruit)
Breeding sitesGarbage, dumpsters, compost, manure, spilled feed, grease traps

Behavior and biology

A female lays batches of eggs in moist decaying material, and in summer heat the cycle from egg to adult can take about a week to ten days, so populations explode quickly. Adults feed by regurgitating and lapping, which is how they move bacteria from waste to food.

Why flies are a problem

In restaurants, food processing and healthcare, flies are an inspection and contamination issue. Around barns and horse properties they stress animals and spread around manure.

Prevention you can do

  • Empty and clean trash cans and dumpsters often; keep lids closed.
  • Remove manure and wet bedding regularly; keep feed areas dry.
  • Repair screens and use door sweeps, air curtains or strip doors at entrances.
  • Clean drains and grease traps on a schedule.
