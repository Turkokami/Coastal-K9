Pharaoh Ant — Identification & Control

The pharaoh ant is a tiny yellowish ant that nests inside heated buildings — apartments, hospitals, senior living, hotels and food service. It is one of the hardest indoor ants to control because the wrong treatment makes it spread.

Quick answer: never spray pharaoh ants. Sprays and repellents trigger "budding," where the colony splits into many new colonies throughout the building. Bait only.

How to identify the pharaoh ant

FeatureWhat to look for
SizeVery small, about 1/16 inch
ColorPale yellow to light amber, with a slightly darker abdomen
WhereIndoors near warmth and moisture: kitchens, bathrooms, wall voids, behind baseboards, in linens and medical supplies
Look-alikesThief ant (similar size, yellow), odorous house ant (dark)

Behavior and biology

Pharaoh ant colonies have many queens and can split at any time, so one building can hold dozens or hundreds of nests. They nest in tiny warm voids — inside walls, electrical boxes, appliances and stacked linens — and trail along pipes and wiring between floors and units.

Why pharaoh ants are a problem

In healthcare settings they have been found in wound dressings, IV equipment and sterile supplies and can carry bacteria. In apartments and hotels they spread from unit to unit through shared walls and plumbing, so treating one unit rarely solves it.

Frequently asked questions

Why did the ants get worse after I sprayed?

Repellent sprays stress the colony, and pharaoh ants respond by budding into multiple new colonies. That's why baits are the only recommended approach.
